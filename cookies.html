What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file a website stores on your device so it can recognise your browser on the next page or the next visit. Related technologies do much the same job: local storage keeps data in the browser, pixels and tracking scripts load a tiny file to record that something happened. This policy covers all of them; we just say "cookies" throughout.

First-party cookies are set by this site. Third-party cookies are set by another company whose content appears on our pages — our forms provider, or an embedded music or video player.

The categories we use

Strictly necessary. The site cannot work properly without these — they keep a form session together, remember your cookie choice, and protect against abuse. They do not need your consent, and you cannot switch them off through us.

Functional. These remember choices you have made so the site behaves the way you left it.

Analytics. These tell us which pages get visited and which links get used, so we can improve the site. They are optional.

Marketing. These would build a profile of your interests to target advertising. We do not currently use any — and if that changes, this page and the Privacy Policy change with it.

Embedded players and fonts

Two things on this site send data to another company even though they are not strictly cookies, and we would rather be straight about them:

Embedded players. The Spotify player and any YouTube video load directly from those companies. That means your IP address and browser details reach them as soon as the page loads, whether or not you press play. If you are signed in to that service, it can connect the visit to your account. Their privacy policies govern what happens next.

Google Fonts. The site's typefaces load from Google's servers, which sends Google your IP address. Google states it does not set cookies for this or use the data for advertising, but the transfer still happens — and a German court has held that serving fonts this way without consent can breach the GDPR. Self-hosting the font files removes the issue entirely.

Your choices

Where the law requires your consent — in the EEA and the UK in particular — non-essential cookies should not be set until you give it, and you should be able to change your mind as easily as you gave it.

Blocking or deleting cookies is always allowed. Strictly necessary ones are the only ones that will affect how the site works, and even then the effect is small: a form may forget what you had typed.

Controlling cookies in your browser

Every major browser lets you see, block and delete cookies. The relevant settings are usually under Privacy or Security:

  • Google Chrome
  • Safari
  • Mozilla Firefox
  • Microsoft Edge

You can also browse in a private or incognito window, which clears cookies when you close it.

Global Privacy Control

If your browser or an extension sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a valid request to opt out of any sale or sharing of your personal information, as California and several other states require. We do not sell or share your data in any case, but the signal is honoured.

Changes to this policy

We will update this page whenever the cookies on the site change — a new embed, an analytics tool, a consent banner. The "last updated" date at the top shows the current version.
